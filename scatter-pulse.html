Scatter Pulse

A wave crossing seven dots. Each one starts at its own moment, timed by how far it sits along the top-left to bottom-right diagonal — so no two move together and the swell reads as travel rather than a blink. The dip is shallow on purpose.

Seeing it in place. Preview loading screen opens a separate window with
  the mark on the app behind a 50% black scrim, at the size it ships at. Your settings stay put —
  keep tuning with the window open and it follows along, restyling in place rather than reloading, so
  the animation never jumps back to the start. Click the button again to bring it forward. If the
  browser blocks the popup it falls back to a full-screen overlay here, closed with escape. Served by
  server.js the window also links through to the real
  /test route with the same parameters, hand-tuned delays included.


  
Hand-tuning. The generated delays are a starting point, not a constraint. Drag any bar in the timeline sideways, or type a number into its delay field, and the mark switches to Manual — your offsets are then stored as fractions of the wave travel, so the travel slider still speeds the whole thing up or down without flattening the shape you set. Switching back to Diagonal leaves the manual version intact to return to. Times CSV exports the per-dot delays, Keyframes CSV the sampled scale curve for pulling into After Effects or a spreadsheet.


  
How the wave is timed. Each dot's delay comes from its own position, not from a group it was sorted into: the centre of every dot is projected onto the top-left to bottom-right diagonal, and that distance — normalised across the mark — scales the wave travel time. The seven dots land on seven different delays, which is what stops it reading as a set of blinks. Every dot then runs identical keyframes at an identical duration and differs only in animation-delay, so the offsets hold on every repeat, not just the first. The curve itself is sampled from smootherstep, with zero velocity at the start, at the bottom of the dip, at the rebound peak and at the end. Transforms only, so it stays on the compositor.
